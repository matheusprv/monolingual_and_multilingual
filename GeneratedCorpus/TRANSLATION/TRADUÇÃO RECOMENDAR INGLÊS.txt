RE: Olive oil

TYPES OF OLIVE OIL

*Refined olive oil: Produced by refining virgin olive oil that has high acidity and defects which are removed during the refining process. It can be blended with virgin olive oil.

*Virgin olive oil: Obtained through mechanical processes. Depending on the acidity of the resulting product, it can be classified as extra virgin, virgin or ordinary. Virgin olive oil has a maximum acidity of 2%.

*Extra virgin olive oil: Must not exceed 0.8% acidity (oleic acid) and must be free of defects. The body that regulates this and defines which defects are classified as such is the International Olive Council (IOC).

*Ordinary olive oil: Obtained by blending lampante oil — unfit for consumption — which has been processed using physico-chemical methods, with virgin and extra virgin olive oil. Ordinary olive oil is not subject to specific regulation.

TIPS FOR CONSUMERS:
When buying the product, it's worth checking the acidity level and the best-before date.

Accepting ageing with grace is the answer, says psychologist Olga Tessari

Old age is a new chapter in a human being's life. New activities, challenges, drastic changes to daily routines and certain limitations are imposed on older people who are not used to the adjustments this stage brings and end up struggling with social and health difficulties. To discuss the subject, we spoke with psychologist and psychotherapist Olga Tessari, who addresses the importance of treating all elderly people with respect, care and warmth.

Father Marcelo Rossi Website – Is the lack of purpose the biggest problem for someone entering old age, and can it even lead to depression?

Olga Tessari – The lack of purpose, combined with the feeling of having no way out of one's current situation, can lead to depression at any age. The particular problem for older people is that they often feel there is no time left to do anything meaningful, because death is drawing closer. On top of that, it's common for them, having more free time, to stop and reflect and conclude that they left many things undone, that they would have liked to do others, and that there is simply no time left. It's important for people to always have goals and pursue their dreams and desires, regardless of age — since we never know when we will die, even though death is the one certainty in life, we must carry on with our plans. It reminds me of a patient of mine who believed her life was over at 50, that there was simply nothing more to do.

I'd been at a company for eight months when a much better opportunity came up. I moved jobs straight away and shortly afterwards was made redundant for no apparent reason. Now, in an interview for a new role, I was asked repeatedly about why I left my last two jobs. My track record gives the impression that I'm a "job hopper". I don't know what to say.

Dear reader, It's true that, on reading your CV, a recruiter may worry that you'll move on quickly again if you're offered the role. But wanting to progress in your career and seize better opportunities when they arise is not something to be ashamed of. You shouldn't feel embarrassed about your history or feel the need to lie by leaving things out. We all have our setbacks and achievements in our professional lives. We all have strengths and areas for development. In the interview, focus on your strengths and show that you're aware of your weaker points and actively working on them. If asked why things "didn't work out at previous employers", explain that the desire to grow and develop professionally drove your decisions. Make it clear it wasn't a whim. Emphasise that the experience taught you to value different aspects of a role and that you now feel more confident taking on new responsibilities. Mention what you took away from each experience and round off your answer with something that showcases your value, so you don't linger on the topic or come across as unsure of yourself. The recruiter will assess many aspects of your profile — knowledge, experience, maturity — and weigh them against the requirements of the role. This won't be the only factor. Don't let it weigh on you too much — play to your strengths. Best of luck!
