     Dear Editor,

     Recent study reveals that one in each three children suffers of bronchial asthma in the United Kingdom1, where the frequency of this disease in adolescents of 16 years grew 70% in the last 12 years. In the biggest cities of Brazil, according results of the International Study of the Asthma and Allergies in the Childhood – ISAAC2, more than 20% of the adolescents of 14 years have asthma. The asthma is not a disease of high lethality, but a chronic infirmity with potential of irreversible damage for the respiratory apparatus. Has high direct cost by the utilization of the health services and medicines, and indirect cost inestimable, by the physical and psychic suffering that imposes to the patients and to its families, with loss in the school or in the work.Fortunately, those that have access to medical attendance of good quality and have resources to implement the preventive and therapeutic cares recommended, count today with drugs extremely effective and safe. These drugs are not capable of cure the asthma, but are enough for: 1) control the dyspnea and the cough; 2) reduce the need of bronchodilators; 3) diminish the hospital admissions; 4) reduce the visits to emergency care; 5) improve the quality of life; 6) allow intense physical activity; 7) reduce the absenteeism in the school and in the work.Unfortunately, however, these medicines, recommended in several consensuses in this decade of 1990, are not accessible to great proportion of the asthmatic Brazilians, whose family income hardly assures them the food and the housing. Are we, therefore before of a critical situation characterized by: 1) high frequency of asthma; 2) growing frequency; 3) unavailability of treatment. Is startling that we have arrived to this point exactly in a phase of great knowledge of the pathogenesis of the allergic inflammation, substrate of the asthma in the majority of the sick, and before of the existence of the corticosteroids for use inhalatory, drugs that revolutionized the individual treatment of the persistent asthma in the last years.
