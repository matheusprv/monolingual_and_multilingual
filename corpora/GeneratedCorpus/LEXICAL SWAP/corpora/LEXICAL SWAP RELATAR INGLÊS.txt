Why work with projects

Clarify your doubts about how to use in classroom the methodology of didactic projects

The specialists guarantee: work with didactic projects is fascinating and surprising. Fascinating for the capacity to involve even the most disengaged students. Surprising for bringing embedded the germ of the unexpected. Next, you will read nine recurring questions about the theme, answered by three great experts on the subject. They are questions that afflict those who have never ventured into this world – and also those who are no longer beginners. Our guests are Regina Scarpa, general coordinator of the Center for Studies and Documentation for Community Action, in São Paulo; Vinicius Ítalo Signorelli, professor of Natural Sciences and member of the program Parameters in Action, from the Ministry of Education; and Vera Grellet, project coordinator at Redeensinar. Many answers are complementary. Others reveal different points of view. Proof that, in education, as you well know, there is no single, crystallized truth.

What does it mean to work with didactic projects?

Vera Grellet It is to start from real and concrete, contextualized questions or situations, that genuinely interest the students. Understanding the problem-situation is the objective of the project. The actions and knowledge necessary for understanding are discussed and planned between the teacher and the students. Everyone has tasks and responsibilities. It is as if it were a journey: we are in São Paulo (what we know) and we want to get to Salvador (the path symbolizes what we will learn). We have to decide what to do, what to take, divide tasks. During the journey, we will also have to make new decisions.

How will the financial crisis affect the Brics?
The crisis has three broad impacts, which affect everyone, but to different degrees. The first is a significant slowdown in the growth of demand for manufactured products, notably in the United States and Europe, and this will have a strong impact on China. The second effect, which affected Russia particularly strongly, but also hit Brazil, is that the financial and banking crisis led to a sharp reduction in credit to emerging economies. This generated downward pressure on the currencies of most of these countries, and almost all had to spend their reserves to compensate for the withdrawal of international bank financing. Russia was in a particularly bad situation, because of the extent to which the Russian private sector borrowed from the international financial system. And finally, there is the large fluctuation of the global economic cycle, which caused a significant correction in commodity prices. This will impact Russia, because of oil, and Brazil, because of iron ore and many other commodities that the country exports.

Are Brazilian reserves sufficient to face this crisis?
Brazilian reserves are substantially larger than its short-term debts. Brazil has the advantage that, in addition to reserves, it now has access to $30 billion from the Fed on the swap line, and probably could draw on the IMF’s new short-term financing instrument. So Brazil has at least $30 billion, and perhaps up to $50 billion, in additional credit.

Enough of magic
V – Your books have spoken less and less of esotericism. Do you still consider yourself a magician?
C – The idea of the magician is much more a matter of perception of the universe. It is a way of looking at the world beyond concrete reality. But I have several books that do not touch on magic. My books speak of philosophical questions.
V – Would this be the common point among them, in your opinion?
C – The common point is something called style. From my first book until now, I have maintained a style that is absolutely direct, lean. I keep cutting, cutting, until I reach the essence of the thing. In the beginning, this was misunderstood. They thought it was something superficial. But it is this characteristic that gives my books their unique aspect.
V – After so much success, do the criticisms still bother you?
C – I am a very polarizing author: people either love me or hate me. I am used to it. But the only criticism that hurt me was not directed at me. It was when they said that my reader was stupid. I do not want to generalize, but there is a cultural fascism in the country.
V – Do you feel persecuted by it?
C – I think that all those who do not fit into a certain standard are persecuted by it, which is to value what is incomprehensible and inaccessible. Only, fortunately, this only applies to critics, who have isolated themselves from reality. The people who write this kind of thing stay in an ivory tower, without knowing what is going on around them. They think they are overwhelming everyone, that everyone is listening to what they say.
