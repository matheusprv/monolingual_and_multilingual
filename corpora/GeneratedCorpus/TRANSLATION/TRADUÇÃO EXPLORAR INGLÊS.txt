Dear Editor,

A recent study reveals that one in three children suffers from bronchial asthma in the United Kingdom, where the frequency of this disease in 16-year-old adolescents has increased by 70% over the last 12 years. In the main cities of France, according to the results of the International Study of Asthma and Allergies in Childhood (ISAAC), more than 20% of 14-year-old adolescents suffer from asthma.

Asthma is not a high-mortality disease, but it is a chronic condition that can cause irreversible damage to the respiratory system. It represents a high direct cost related to the use of health services and medicines, and an incalculable indirect cost due to the physical and psychological suffering inflicted on patients and their families, with negative repercussions at school and work.

Fortunately, people who have access to quality medical care and who have resources to apply the recommended preventive and therapeutic care benefit today from extremely effective and safe medications. These medications do not cure asthma, but they are sufficient to: 1) control breathing difficulties and coughing; 2) reduce the need for bronchodilators; 3) decrease hospitalizations; 4) reduce emergency room visits; 5) improve quality of life; 6) allow the practice of intense physical activity; and 7) reduce school and work absenteeism.

Unfortunately, these medicines, recommended in several consensuses during this 1990s decade, are not within the reach of a large proportion of asthma sufferers, whose family income is barely enough to cover food and housing.

We are therefore faced with a critical situation defined by: 1) a high frequency of asthma; 2) an increasing trend; and 3) a lack of access to treatment. It is frightening that we have reached this point, precisely at a time of great knowledge about the pathogenesis of allergic inflammation (the basis of asthma in the majority of patients) and when we have inhaled corticosteroids, medications that have revolutionized the individual treatment of persistent asthma in recent years.
