How do I learn to do my own makeup without taking a course? What do I need to buy to get started?

Look, start with the basics — that's what I did. Stock up on the essentials: foundation, powder, eyeshadow in two shades (a light one and a darker one), mascara, eyeliner, and a lip gloss close to your natural lip color. I don't use foundation myself, but you might need it: with clean skin, spread the foundation evenly over your whole face (when you buy it, ask the salesperson to help you pick the right shade — that's really important!); apply compact powder using the same logic for the shade; then spread the dark eyeshadow over the eyelid, following the curve of your eye to form a half-moon shape; next, apply the light eyeshadow (highlighter) under your eyebrow and across the space between your eyebrow and the dark shadow; blend with a makeup brush (pick one up too); apply the eyeliner — brown is best — right along the upper lash line; put on mascara; use an eyelash curler (grab one of those as well); apply another coat of mascara, and you're done.

How to solve a math problem without using math?

Posted by Oscar Y. Takeshita

One technique that often comes in handy is converting a given problem into an equivalent one. This is especially useful when the second problem is much easier to solve or understand. Once you solve the second problem, you automatically have the solution to the first.

Let's illustrate this with a classic problem recently posted on this blog.

The standard way to solve this problem relies on conditional probability. The solution below barely touches probability at all.

Suppose there's another TV show where you have three doors, one of which hides a prize car. The rules, however, are different:
1. There is no host.
2. You get to choose one of the following:
a) Open two doors of your choosing.
b) Open one door of your choosing.

Which would you pick? Obviously option a).

Ask the captain.

"On a flight from Foz do Iguaçu, I noticed something: the tip of the 737-800NG's wing curves upward. What's that for?"

Those upward-curved extensions at the wingtips are called winglets, and they improve the aircraft's aerodynamics. Although they represent a major breakthrough in aeronautical engineering, this design has existed in nature for millions of years. Look at large birds like condors, eagles, and vultures — they have feathers that curve up at the tips of their wings. This allows air to flow more smoothly over the wings and enables the birds to glide for extended periods while burning as little energy as possible, whether they're hunting for food or defending their territory.

1 – What is FIES?
FIES is a Ministry of Education program designed to help finance college education for students enrolled in private (fee-paying) institutions. It was created in 1999, and as of this year it operates under significant changes that make the application process even more accessible for students.

2 – Who can apply for FIES?
Students regularly enrolled in non-free undergraduate programs that have received a positive evaluation from SINAES and are offered by a higher education institution participating in the Program are eligible to apply for FIES funding.

A student may only apply for funding for a single program in which they are currently enrolled. Students whose academic enrollment has been placed on a full course suspension during the FIES application period are not considered regularly enrolled.
